The Problem

Before touching any technology, understand the broken process. Here's a realistic sales discovery workflow at a mid-market B2B software company — with all the dysfunction that leads to misaligned proposals and lost deals.

▶

Video walkthrough coming soon

The scenario: Meridian Solutions

Company: Mid-market B2B workforce management platform
Modules: Time & Attendance, Scheduling, Compliance, Analytics
Verticals: Healthcare, manufacturing, professional services
Deal size: $75K–$400K ACV, 4–8 month sales cycles

Phases 1–4: How discovery information gets gathered

Phase 1
Lead handoff
SDR qualifies lead with loose BANT. Hands off via Salesforce task + Slack. No structured template. No minimum info threshold.
→
Phase 2
Discovery call
AE runs discovery. MEDDIC adopted but inconsistent. MEDDIC fields sparsely populated. Notes are free text.
→
Phase 3
Stakeholder assessment
AE meets 2–3 of 5–8 stakeholders. Fills gaps with assumptions. No stakeholder mapping template.
→
Phase 4
Technical scoping
SE logs findings in Confluence, not Salesforce. Scope doc emailed as Word file. SE re-asks questions prospect already answered.
All of this feeds into Phase 5 — where things break

Phase 5: Needs-to-proposal translation (detailed)

5.1
Account Executive
AE gathers discovery information to build proposal. Must pull from multiple sources and synthesize manually.
SalesforceConfluenceEmailGongMemory
Pain point

Discovery data lives in 5 places: SDR notes in Salesforce, AE's call notes in Salesforce, SE's Confluence doc, SE's emailed Word scope doc, and Gong recordings nobody re-watches. No single consolidated view.

5.2
Account Executive
AE selects product modules and builds commercial proposal based on what they can piece together.
Pain point

Translation is lossy. AE defaults to "standard" vertical package. Recommends modules prospect didn't need. Misses critical modules (e.g. Compliance). Underprices by $40K+ because SE's scope doc flagged a custom API build the AE didn't read.

5.3
Account Executive → Prospect
Send proposal to prospect. Prospect pushes back on misalignment.
Pain point

"You recommended modules we didn't ask for." "You missed our compliance requirement." "Your pricing doesn't include the integration your own SE scoped." Credibility damaged.

5.4
Account Executive
Re-enter discovery. Second round of calls that should have happened the first time. Adds weeks to deal cycle.

Redesign

The Redesign

Don't bolt AI onto a broken process. Fix the process first. The OPS framework addresses three categories of failure that are independently good practice — and that create the conditions AI needs to function downstream.

▶

Video walkthrough coming soon

O
One source of truth
All discovery data consolidated into a single system. If it's not there, it doesn't exist for the purposes of this deal.
P
Process handoffs
Structured templates replace verbal recaps and ad-hoc notes. Required fields, not free text.
S
Stage gates
Data is validated before it progresses. Deals can't advance without meeting minimum quality thresholds.

Why these three? They're not random fixes. They're the three that are independently justified as good operational practice and that create the prerequisites for AI to work: clean inputs, consolidated context, and validated data to reason from.

Phase 5 — Redesigned with OPS fixes
5.1
Account Executive
AE opens single consolidated deal record.
O — One source of truth

All discovery data — SDR handoff, AE notes, MEDDIC fields, stakeholder map, SE technical scope — lives in one record. No hunting across Confluence, email, or Gong.

5.2
Account Executive + Solutions Engineer
AE uses structured discovery-to-proposal template.
P — Process handoff

Template maps each stakeholder need to a product module, flags unconfirmed assumptions, and cross-references SE technical scope. SE's findings are already in the same record.

5.3
Account Executive
AE builds proposal from complete, consolidated record.
5.4
Account Executive + Sales Manager
Proposal review before sending.
S — Stage gate

Does the configuration map to documented needs? Are SE-flagged requirements reflected in pricing? Are assumptions called out? Manager reviews structured data, not the AE's verbal summary.

5.5
Account Executive → Prospect
Send proposal to prospect.

Analysis

Why Chain-of-Thought

The OPS fixes solved the data and process problems. But translating a complete discovery record into the right product configuration is still genuinely hard — it requires multi-step reasoning, not a checklist.

▶

Video walkthrough coming soon

Chain-of-Thought in one line: Give the model all the inputs upfront and have it work through them step by step, showing its reasoning at every stage — so the output isn't just an answer, it's a defensible argument.

Where CoT applies in Phase 5
5.3a
Chain-of-Thought step 1
Pain-to-module mapping
Chain-of-Thought

Map each documented stakeholder pain to a product module. Reason step by step: "VP Ops stated compliance risk as top concern → maps to Compliance Tracking module. IT flagged HIPAA requirements → confirms Compliance, adds healthcare configuration."

5.3b
Chain-of-Thought step 2
Constraint reconciliation
Chain-of-Thought

Reconcile competing constraints: "SE scoped a custom API at $45K. HR's budget ceiling is $220K. Standard 4-module package is $185K. With integration, we exceed budget → either reduce scope or propose phased implementation."

5.3c
Chain-of-Thought step 3
Configuration recommendation with visible reasoning
Chain-of-Thought

Produce final recommendation. Every module included or excluded has a documented rationale tied to a specific discovery finding. The reasoning chain is inspectable — a manager can audit it, a junior AE can learn from it.

Why CoT is the right concept here

All inputs are available upfront

Unlike a live conversation (which would require ReAct), the proposal step has everything: the full discovery record, the product catalog, the pricing. CoT works through what's already there.

Multi-step reasoning, not a lookup

The right configuration isn't a one-to-one mapping. It requires weighing competing needs, reconciling budget with technical requirements, and making trade-off decisions.

The reasoning is the value

A junior AE could guess at a configuration. The value is showing why — tying every recommendation to a discovery finding. That's what makes the proposal defensible.

Visible reasoning solves tribal knowledge

Today, top AEs' reasoning is invisible. CoT externalizes it into a chain that can be audited, reviewed, and studied. The organization retains the logic even if the AE leaves.

Setup: How the Claude project works

1

Create a Claude project

A project gives Claude persistent knowledge — the product catalog, pricing, and commercial policy that a senior AE would have internalized.

2

Upload the product catalog

Meridian's four modules, pricing tiers, vertical packages, integration costs, and discount policy. This is what Claude reasons against.

3

Write the system prompt

Tells Claude its role (senior sales consultant) and the five-step reasoning process: pain-to-module mapping, constraint reconciliation, trade-off analysis, configuration recommendation, risk callout.

4

Paste the discovery record

A consolidated deal file for Bayside Regional Medical Center — a $200K+ healthcare deal with a Cerner integration that blows up the budget, a union environment, an unmet CFO, and a late-arriving procurement process.

The discovery record: Bayside Regional Medical Center

Deal snapshot

Company: Bayside Regional Medical Center
Vertical: Healthcare, 2,200 employees, 3 facilities
Est. deal: $200K–$250K ACV
Timeline: 6 months (Kronos contract expiring)
Champion: Maria Chen, Director of HR
Budget ceiling: $220K (could stretch to $250K)

What makes this deal interesting for CoT

🔴
Budget conflict: The Cerner EHR integration requires a custom bidirectional API build at $45K + $8K/year. Combined with the full 4-module healthcare package, this pushes the deal past the $220K comfort zone.
🔴
Union complexity: Facility 2 has 230 unionized nurses, requiring the union rule compliance add-on for scheduling — another $8K/year.
🟡
Unmet stakeholder: CFO James Park (final approver above $200K) has not been spoken with directly. Maria believes he'll approve — but it's an assumption.
🟡
Late procurement: If the deal exceeds $200K, procurement issues a formal RFP. The procurement contact hasn't been identified. Timeline risk.
🟡
Scope uncertainty: Facility 3 may have a different EHR than Cerner. If true, integration scope expands. Two department managers (Lab, Radiology) haven't been contacted.

What to watch for in Claude's output

Claude's extended thinking shows the step-by-step reasoning chain — you can expand it to see the logic. The final output should map every stakeholder pain to a module, catch the budget conflict from the Cerner integration, flag the unmet CFO and late procurement as risks, and produce a configuration recommendation where every choice ties back to a specific discovery finding. A junior AE would miss most of this. The CoT reasoning catches all of it — and shows its work.
